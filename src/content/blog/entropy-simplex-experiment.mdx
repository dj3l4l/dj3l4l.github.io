This page is an experiment for the new version of the site. It asks a simple design question: can a mathematical diagram become something closer to a scientific instrument?

For a distribution over three outcomes,

$$
p=(p_1,p_2,p_3), \qquad p_1+p_2+p_3=1,
$$

every possible distribution is a point in a triangle. Its Shannon entropy is

$$
H(p)=-\sum_{i=1}^{3}p_i\log p_i.
$$

<EntropyExplorer client:visible />

Move the three controls. The marker shows the resulting distribution inside the probability simplex, while the numerical display reports its entropy. Equal probabilities place the marker in the centre and maximise the entropy at $\log 3$ nats. Moving towards a corner makes one outcome increasingly certain, so the entropy falls.

The component above is hydrated only when it approaches the viewport. The surrounding article remains ordinary static HTML, including the equations. That is the central architectural idea behind this migration: an essay should pay the JavaScript cost of an interactive figure, not the cost of becoming an application in its entirety.
